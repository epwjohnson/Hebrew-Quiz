חֲנֻכָּה
The Festival of Lights

Chanukah commemorates the rededication of the Temple in Jerusalem and the miracle of the oil that burned for eight days. Candles are lit at nightfall on each of the eight nights, with blessings recited before lighting.

Three blessings are said, in order:

1. Lehadlik Ner — To kindle the light
Said on all eight nights. The blessing for the commandment of lighting the Chanukah candles.
2. She'asa Nisim — Who performed miracles
Said on all eight nights. A blessing of thanksgiving for the miracles performed for our ancestors in those days at this season.
3. Shehecheyanu — Who has kept us alive
Said on the first night only. A blessing of gratitude for reaching this season, recited whenever we perform a mitzvah for the first time in the year.
בְּרָכוֹת
Blessings

A brachah, a blessing, is a short way of stopping to notice a moment and give thanks for it: on waking, before and after eating, when doing a mitzvah, or when something wonderful, or terrible, happens.

One pattern. Most blessings open with the same six words, "Blessed are You, Lord our G_d, Ruler of the universe". Learn them once and you can read almost any blessing: only the ending changes.

בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה יְהוָה אֱלֹהֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם

Blessings for a mitzvah. When a blessing comes before a commandment, such as lighting candles or putting on a tallit, it adds "who has made us holy with Your commandments and commanded us".

אֲשֶׁר קִדְּשָׁנוּ בְּמִצְוֹתָיו וְצִוָּנוּ

Answering. When someone else says a blessing, we answer "Amen", אָמֵן "so be it".
